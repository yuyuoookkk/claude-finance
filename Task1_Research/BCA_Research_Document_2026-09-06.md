## 1. COMPANY OVERVIEW

PT Bank Central Asia Tbk ("BCA") is Indonesia's largest private-sector bank and, by market capitalisation, the largest listed company in the country. It is a domestic universal bank: it takes deposits from Indonesian households and businesses, lends to Indonesian corporates, small businesses and consumers, and operates the payments infrastructure through which a very large share of Indonesian commerce settles. It has essentially no international franchise, and that concentration is deliberate.

The bank's defining characteristic is its funding base. As of FY2025, BCA held approximately Rp1,249tn of third-party funds, of which roughly Rp1,045tn — about 84.6% — sat in current accounts and savings accounts (CASA) rather than time deposits. By the first half of 2026, CASA had grown 10.2% year-on-year to approximately Rp1,082tn. This is an extraordinary ratio by any international standard, and it is the single most important fact about the company. CASA balances are cheap and, for a transaction bank, sticky: customers hold them not to earn interest but because the account is the one they use to pay for things. The result is a structurally low cost of funds that allows BCA to lend to Indonesia's highest-quality corporate borrowers at competitive rates while still earning a net interest margin that most banks could only achieve by lending to riskier customers.

That funding advantage is not an accident of history; it is manufactured daily by transaction volume. In 2025 BCA processed approximately 42 billion transactions, up 17% year-on-year, with peak days approaching 300 million transactions. Mobile and internet banking transaction frequency grew 19% year-on-year. The bank serves more than 43 million customer accounts through roughly 1,270 branches and 20,163 ATMs, supported by over 27,000 employees. The branch and ATM estate is expensive to maintain and, in an era of digital banking, superficially looks like a legacy liability. In BCA's case it functions as the physical anchor of the deposit franchise, particularly for the small-business customers whose operating accounts are the most valuable CASA of all.

**Scale and performance (FY2025).** Total assets were approximately Rp1,587tn. Loans stood at approximately Rp993tn at year-end 2025. Net profit was approximately Rp57.5–58tn, up roughly 4.9% year-on-year — a deceleration from the double-digit growth of prior years, and an important signal discussed below. Return on equity was in the low-to-mid 20s; sources differ on the precise figure (see Section 10), with 24.1% for FY2025 against 24.8% for FY2024 reported by one sell-side source and lower figures from data aggregators using different equity definitions. Asset quality was strong and improving: the NPL ratio was 1.7% and loan-at-risk (LAR) improved to 4.8% from 5.3% the prior year.

**Current trading (1H 2026).** The most recent reported period shows a franchise under margin pressure but with resilient volumes. Net profit for the first half of 2026 was approximately Rp29.5tn, up about 2% year-on-year — barely above inflation, and the clearest evidence that BCA's earnings algorithm has shifted. Loans crossed Rp1,000tn for the first time in the bank's history, reaching approximately Rp1,036tn, up 8% year-on-year. The growth was concentrated in corporate lending, up 13.6% year-on-year to approximately Rp513.4tn and now representing about 49.6% of the book; commercial and SME lending grew 6.6% to approximately Rp288.5tn. Consumer lending contracted, dragged by a roughly 20% year-on-year decline in auto loans, partly offset by mortgages up about 5% and personal loans up about 7%.

Net interest margin compressed to approximately 5.3% in 1H26 from 5.8% a year earlier — a 50 basis point decline that, on a book of this size, is the difference between mid-single-digit and low-single-digit earnings growth. Asset quality softened modestly, with NPL at approximately 1.9% and LAR at approximately 4.9%, both up slightly from FY2025.

**The investment question.** BCA's economics are simple to state and difficult to replicate: the cheapest funding in Indonesian banking, deployed conservatively, producing a sector-leading return on equity with sector-leading asset quality. The market has always paid for this — BBCA has historically traded at a substantial premium to every domestic peer on price-to-book. The question for an initiation is not whether the franchise is good. It plainly is. The question is whether the price already reflects it, and whether the 1H26 combination of NIM compression, negligible profit growth, and deliberately sub-guidance loan growth represents a cyclical trough or the beginning of structural normalisation. Those are Task 3 questions, but the qualitative groundwork for answering them is laid in Sections 6 through 9 below.

## 2. COMPANY HISTORY

BCA's history divides cleanly into three eras, and the boundary events — the 1998 collapse and the 2002 change of control — explain much of the bank's present-day conservatism.

**Founding and the Salim era (1957–1998).** Bank Central Asia was founded on 21 February 1957 by Sudono Salim, known also by his Chinese name Liem Sioe Liong. Salim was the architect of what became the Salim Group, the largest Indonesian conglomerate of the Suharto period, with interests spanning flour milling, cement, instant noodles, automotive distribution and property. BCA was, in practical terms, the group's financial arm. Through the 1970s and 1980s it grew alongside the Indonesian economy and alongside the Salim Group's own expansion, building a retail branch network and a reputation for reliable payments service that outlasted everything else about the era.

By the mid-1990s BCA was among the largest private banks in Indonesia. Its lending, however, carried the structural flaw common to conglomerate-affiliated banks throughout Asia at the time: a large proportion of the loan book was extended to companies connected to its owner. Related-party concentration of this kind is invisible while asset prices rise and catastrophic when they fall.

**Crisis, state control, and recapitalisation (1998–2002).** The Asian Financial Crisis reached Indonesia in 1997 and became a political crisis in 1998. As the Suharto government fell, depositors moved against banks associated with the regime and its business allies. BCA suffered a run: withdrawals exceeded Rp30tn in early 1998. Having borrowed from Bank Indonesia at levels reported to exceed 200% of its capital, the bank could not meet redemptions. On 29 May 1998 the government assumed control through IBRA, the Indonesian Bank Restructuring Agency.

The rescue was expensive. Recapitalisation involved injecting approximately Rp60tn of government bonds into the bank, with total state support reported at approximately Rp87.99tn. By 1999 IBRA held 92.8% of the equity. BCA serviced and ultimately retired the recapitalisation bonds, completing the process by 2009 — a detail worth noting, because it means the modern balance sheet carries no residue of the rescue.

BCA returned to the public market on 31 May 2000, when IBRA sold 22.5% of the shares in an IPO, reducing the state holding to approximately 70.3%. The listing established BBCA on what was then the Jakarta Stock Exchange.

**The Hartono era (2002–present).** In 2002 IBRA divested its controlling block. The buyer was FarIndo Investments, the acquisition vehicle of Robert Budi Hartono and Michael Bambang Hartono — brothers whose principal asset was Djarum, one of Indonesia's largest kretek (clove cigarette) manufacturers. FarIndo acquired approximately 51.15% of the bank. The holding was subsequently restructured through PT Dwimuria Investama Andalan, which by 2017 held 54.94%, the level maintained since. Robert Budi Hartono is identified as the ultimate controlling shareholder; public shareholders hold the balance, approximately 45.06% as of December 2024.

The significance of the ownership change is cultural as much as financial. The Hartono family took control of a bank that had been destroyed once by related-party lending, and did not repeat the pattern. Djarum is not a large borrower from BCA. The bank was run instead as a standalone commercial enterprise with an explicit strategic emphasis on transaction banking and low-cost deposits — the franchise that had survived the crisis — rather than on aggressive balance-sheet growth. The conservatism visible in BCA's underwriting today, and its consistently low NPL ratio relative to Indonesian peers, traces directly to this period.

**Recent developments (2020–2026).** Through the 2020s BCA built out a digital stack alongside the branch network: BCA mobile, KlikBCA internet banking, the Sakuku e-money product, and from 2021 the consolidated myBCA application. It established Bank Digital BCA, operating the consumer digital bank *blu*, as a separate entity targeting a younger, digitally-native customer segment — a defensive move against the fintech-backed digital banks discussed in Section 7. *blu* reported net profit growth of 134.5% in 2024 to Rp108bn, small in group context but directionally significant.

The most consequential recent event is the leadership transition of 2025. Jahja Setiaatmadja, President Director since 2011 and the executive most identified with BCA's modern strategy, moved to the role of President Commissioner. Gregory Hendra Lembong, previously Deputy President Director with responsibility for IT and operations, became President Director on 12 March 2025. A CEO succession at a bank whose valuation premium rests substantially on management consistency is a material event for an initiation, and is treated as such in Sections 3 and 9.

In July 2026 the bank passed a symbolic milestone, with loans exceeding Rp1,000tn for the first time.

## 3. MANAGEMENT TEAM

BCA's governance structure follows the Indonesian two-tier model: a Board of Commissioners (*Dewan Komisaris*) with supervisory responsibility, and a Board of Directors (*Direksi*) with executive responsibility. The President Director is the chief executive. Note that under this structure the President Commissioner is a supervisory chair, not an executive chairman.

### Gregory Hendra Lembong — President Director

Hendra Lembong became President Director of BCA on 12 March 2025, succeeding Jahja Setiaatmadja. He is 53 and brings over 30 years of banking experience across Indonesian and international institutions — a background materially different from that of his predecessor, who was a career BCA insider.

Before joining BCA he held senior positions at CIMB Niaga and CIMB Group in Malaysia, and earlier at J.P. Morgan Asia Pacific, Deutsche Bank in London, and Citibank. That combination — Indonesian domestic banking plus international wholesale and investment banking — is unusual in the Indonesian market, where senior bank management is typically drawn from purely domestic careers. He joined BCA and was appointed Deputy President Director in 2022, with oversight of strategic information technology and operations, a portfolio that gave him direct responsibility for the bank's digital transformation and its transaction-processing backbone in the three years before his elevation.

His stated areas of expertise span change management, banking strategy, treasury, accounting and financial management, corporate banking, international banking, risk management, and digital banking. As President Director he holds general supervision over the Director of Finance & Corporate Planning and the Director of Banking Transactions. His executive education includes the Pacific Rim Bankers Program at the University of Washington Foster School (2012), the ASEAN Global Leadership Program at UC Berkeley (2017), and a program at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business (2023).

*Data-quality note: some online profiles of Lembong contain career details — an accountancy role at Price Waterhouse beginning 1979, a finance directorship at PT Kalbe Farma from 1980 to 1989, and a 1982 undergraduate degree — that are chronologically incompatible with his stated age of 53 and appear to be contaminated by another individual's biography. Those details are excluded here and should not be relied upon. His education history requires verification against BCA's own annual report disclosure before use in the final report.*

**Assessment.** The elevation of an IT-and-operations executive with international wholesale experience, rather than a retail-banking lifer, is a legible strategic signal: BCA appears to be positioning for a period in which technology delivery and wholesale-banking depth matter more than branch expansion. The transition risk is real but mitigated by his three years inside the bank and by Setiaatmadja's continued presence in the supervisory chair. Eighteen months into the tenure, the 1H26 results show the franchise operating normally, with no evidence of strategic discontinuity — though also no evidence yet of a distinctive new direction.

### Armand Wahyudi Hartono — Deputy President Director

Armand Wahyudi Hartono has served as Deputy President Director since 2016, having previously been a Director of the bank and, before that, Head of Regional Planning and Development. He is a member of the controlling family — the son of Robert Budi Hartono — which makes his role structurally important beyond its formal responsibilities.

He holds an undergraduate degree from the University of California, San Diego and a graduate degree from Stanford University. Prior to BCA he served as Finance Director of PT Djarum, the family's principal operating business.

**Assessment.** Family representation in senior executive management is a double-edged governance feature. On one side, it aligns day-to-day management with the interests of a controlling shareholder whose horizon is generational rather than quarterly — plausibly a contributor to BCA's willingness to sacrifice near-term loan growth for underwriting discipline, as visible in the sub-guidance 2026 lending. On the other, it constrains the independence of the executive board and creates a succession dynamic not governed purely by merit. For a bank whose premium valuation rests on governance quality, this deserves explicit treatment in the final report rather than omission. The relevant empirical point is that under Hartono family control since 2002, BCA has not repeated the related-party lending that destroyed it under its previous conglomerate owner — the governance record supports the family's stewardship.

### Vera Eve Lim — Director, Finance & Corporate Planning (CFO)

Vera Eve Lim is Director of Finance and Corporate Planning, functioning as Chief Financial Officer with responsibility for directing and overseeing financial activities across the bank and group. She has over 20 years of experience in banking and financial institutions, with functional breadth across accounting, tax, investor relations, corporate planning and strategy, and mergers and acquisitions.

Before joining BCA she was Director and CFO of Bank Danamon from 2006 to 2017, a tenure spanning the global financial crisis and its Indonesian aftermath. She concurrently held board positions in the Danamon group: Commissioner of Adira Finance from 2010 to 2017, and Deputy President Commissioner of Adira Insurance from 2008 to 2013. Adira is one of Indonesia's largest multi-finance companies, concentrated in automotive and consumer financing.

**Assessment.** The Adira background is directly relevant to BCA's current situation. Auto lending is the single weakest part of BCA's consumer book, down roughly 20% year-on-year in 1Q26, and Lim's decade of board-level exposure to Indonesia's largest auto-finance franchise gives the finance function unusually specific insight into that cycle. Her investor-relations remit also matters for an initiation: BCA's disclosure quality and the consistency of its guidance are inputs to the valuation premium, and both fall within her responsibility. Eleven-plus years at a competitor CFO role before joining provides genuine external benchmarking perspective.

### Jahja Setiaatmadja — President Commissioner

Though no longer an executive, Setiaatmadja warrants inclusion because of his continuing influence. He served as President Director from 2011 until March 2025 — a fourteen-year tenure during which BCA's market capitalisation multiplied several times over and the bank consolidated its position as Indonesia's premier transaction bank. He moved to President Commissioner in 2025.

**Assessment.** His retention in the supervisory chair is the principal mitigant to CEO-transition risk. It preserves institutional memory and signals continuity to a shareholder base that has long treated management stability as part of the investment case. The countervailing governance consideration is standard and should be noted: a former long-serving CEO chairing the board that supervises his successor can constrain the successor's freedom to change direction. Whether this is a live constraint at BCA is not observable from outside; it is worth monitoring through the pace of strategic change under Lembong.

## 4. PRODUCTS & SERVICES

BCA is a universal bank, but its product portfolio is best understood as one primary franchise — transaction banking and deposit gathering — with a lending business attached to it, rather than the reverse. This ordering is unusual and is the source of the bank's economics.

### Deposits and transaction banking

The core product is the everyday transaction account. BCA offers current accounts and savings accounts across retail and business segments, and the resulting CASA balances — approximately Rp1,082tn as of 1H26, roughly 84.6% of third-party funds at FY25 — constitute the bank's principal competitive asset. Time deposits form the remaining balance and are priced as a marginal funding source rather than a core one.

What makes the CASA sticky is the payments utility wrapped around it. BCA's transaction infrastructure — 20,163 ATMs, approximately 1,270 branches, and the digital channels described below — handled roughly 42 billion transactions in 2025. For Indonesian consumers and, critically, for small and medium businesses, a BCA account is the default settlement rail. Payroll, supplier payments, tax remittances, e-commerce settlement and interbank transfers run through it. That utility value, rather than deposit rates, is what retains the balance. It is the reason BCA can hold deposits at rates well below what SeaBank or Bank Jago offer and lose relatively little volume.

### Digital channels

The digital estate comprises **myBCA**, the consolidated flagship application launched to unify the bank's retail digital experience; **BCA mobile**, the earlier and still widely used mobile banking app; **KlikBCA**, internet banking, oriented toward business users; and **Sakuku**, an e-money product. Transaction frequency across mobile and internet banking grew 19% year-on-year in 2025, outpacing total transaction growth of 17% and confirming continued channel migration.

Separately, **blu by BCA Digital** operates as a standalone digital bank through the Bank Digital BCA subsidiary. It targets younger, digitally-native customers who might otherwise be captured by fintech-backed challengers, and allows BCA to compete on digital-native product design without repricing its main deposit base. *blu* grew net profit 134.5% in 2024 to Rp108bn — immaterial to group earnings, material as a strategic hedge.

### Lending

The loan book stood at approximately Rp1,036tn at 1H26, composed as follows:

| Segment | 1H26 balance | YoY growth | Share of book |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corporate | ~Rp513.4tn | +13.6% | ~49.6% |
| Commercial & SME | ~Rp288.5tn | +6.6% | ~27.8% |
| Consumer (mortgage, auto, personal, cards) | ~Rp234tn (residual) | negative | ~22.6% |

The corporate book is the growth engine and has become the plurality of lending. BCA lends to Indonesia's largest and best-rated corporates, and its funding cost advantage is what allows it to win that business at spreads that still clear its return hurdle. Commercial and SME lending is the historic core and remains strategically central, because SME operating accounts generate the highest-value CASA.

Consumer lending is the weak segment. Mortgages (KPR) grew approximately 5% year-on-year in 1Q26 and personal loans approximately 7%, but auto loans contracted approximately 20% year-on-year, dragging the segment negative. The auto decline reflects an Indonesian vehicle-sales downturn rather than a BCA-specific credit event, but it removes a historically profitable, high-yield component of the asset mix — one of the mechanical contributors to NIM compression.

### Subsidiaries

BCA operates a group of financial subsidiaries that extend the customer relationship beyond banking: **BCA Finance** (vehicle and consumer multi-finance), **Bank BCA Syariah** (Islamic banking), **Bank Digital BCA** (*blu*), **BCA Sekuritas** (securities brokerage and capital markets), **Asuransi Umum BCA** (general insurance), **Asuransi Jiwa BCA / BCA Life** (life insurance), and **Central Capital Ventura** (corporate venture capital, providing fintech optionality and early visibility on competitive threats).

The stated strategy is cross-group coordination to raise lifetime customer value — routing an existing deposit customer into a mortgage, an insurance policy, a brokerage account. The subsidiaries are individually small against the bank but collectively raise switching costs and diversify fee income. Their precise earnings contributions require extraction from the annual report for Task 2.

### Fee income

Fee and commission income — payments, cards, transfers, remittances, wealth and bancassurance — is a meaningful and comparatively stable revenue stream that scales with transaction volume rather than with balance-sheet size or interest rates. In a compressing-margin environment, fee income growth is one of the few levers available to offset NII pressure, and its trajectory is a key monitoring item. Exact figures require primary-source extraction.

## 5. CUSTOMERS & GO-TO-MARKET

### Customer base

BCA serves more than 43 million customer accounts, a figure that on its own overstates unique customers, since individuals frequently hold multiple accounts. The base spans three broad segments.

**Retail consumers** represent the largest number of accounts and the foundation of the savings franchise. These are mass-market and mass-affluent Indonesians for whom BCA is the primary bank. Individually the balances are small; in aggregate they are the cheapest funding in the system.

**Small and medium enterprises** are the strategically decisive segment. An SME's operating account carries higher average balances than a retail account, generates dense transaction flow (and therefore fee income), and is markedly harder to move — switching a business's settlement account means reconfiguring payroll, suppliers, and customer collections. This segment is where BCA's branch network earns its cost.

**Corporates** are the fastest-growing lending segment, up 13.6% year-on-year, and now roughly half the loan book. The relationship typically extends beyond credit into cash management, payroll, treasury, and trade finance — services that generate fee income and, importantly, corporate current-account balances that feed back into CASA.

### Distribution

Distribution is deliberately hybrid. The physical network of approximately 1,270 branches and 20,163 ATMs is not maintained out of institutional inertia; it is the acquisition and servicing channel for the SME and mass-market deposit base, and its geographic reach across the archipelago is a genuine barrier to entry. Replicating it is prohibitively expensive, and for a bank whose product is trust in a country that experienced a banking collapse within living memory, physical presence retains real value.

Layered on top is the digital estate, which now carries the overwhelming majority of transaction volume. The economic logic is that digital channels handle transactions at near-zero marginal cost while branches handle acquisition, complex service, and business relationships. Transaction volumes growing 17% annually against a broadly stable branch count is the operating leverage this design produces.

The Halo BCA contact centre operates 24 hours, and *blu* provides a fully digital acquisition channel for customers who will never enter a branch.

### Go-to-market and partnerships

BCA's customer acquisition is substantially organic and reputational — a function of network ubiquity and the perception of reliability — rather than driven by rate competition or marketing spend. This is a structural advantage: the bank does not need to buy deposits.

Ecosystem and wholesale partnerships are an increasing focus, with the bank reported to be deepening wholesale and ecosystem banking as a strategic priority. Merchant acquiring, payroll mandates, supply-chain finance and e-commerce settlement all serve the same end: embedding BCA in transaction flows that generate both fee income and float.

Customer concentration risk is low on the deposit side by construction — 43 million accounts — and moderate on the lending side given corporate concentration at roughly half the book. Single-name and sector exposures within the corporate portfolio require extraction from the annual report; this is a genuine analytical gap and matters for the credit-cost assumptions in Task 2.

## 6. INDUSTRY OVERVIEW

### Definition and structure

BCA competes in Indonesian commercial banking, regulated by OJK with monetary policy set by Bank Indonesia. OJK classifies banks by core capital into KBMI tiers, with KBMI 4 — core capital above Rp70tn — comprising the largest institutions: BCA, Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BBRI), Bank Mandiri (BMRI), and Bank Negara Indonesia (BBNI). These four dominate the system.

The structure is a concentrated oligopoly with a distinctive feature: three of the four largest banks are state-controlled. BBRI, BMRI and BBNI are majority government-owned; BCA is the only privately controlled member of the top tier. This matters commercially and politically. State banks carry policy obligations — directed lending to priority sectors, participation in government programmes — that BCA does not. BCA's freedom to decline unattractive lending is a structural advantage, visible in its willingness to run 2026 loan growth below its own guidance while state peers grew far faster.

### Macroeconomic backdrop

Indonesia is Southeast Asia's largest economy, and the operating environment in 2026 is broadly supportive but decelerating at the margin. Bank Indonesia projects GDP growth of approximately 5.33% for 2026, potentially reaching 5.4% if fiscal spending accelerates; first-quarter 2026 growth came in at 5.61% year-on-year, ahead of that projection. Core inflation is projected at approximately 2.62%.

Bank Indonesia held its policy rate at 4.75% through early 2026 — five consecutive meetings by January — with the hold explicitly motivated by rupiah weakness rather than domestic inflation. The rupiah is expected around Rp16,430 per US dollar. This is the central tension in the current environment: BI would prefer to cut to support growth but is constrained by currency pressure, leaving policy rates higher than the domestic cycle alone would warrant.

Fiscal policy is expansionary under the Prabowo administration, with the OECD expecting the budget deficit to widen to approximately 3.0% of GDP in 2026, driven by fuel subsidies and the Free Nutritious Meal programme. Danantara, the sovereign wealth vehicle, represents potential upside through accelerated capital deployment.

### Credit growth and margin dynamics

System loan growth was approximately 9.69% in 2025, within both BI's 8–11% forecast and OJK's 9–11% expectation. Growth eased to approximately 9.37% year-on-year in February 2026 from 9.96% the prior month. BI targets 8–12% credit growth for 2026.

The defining industry dynamic in 2026 is margin compression. BCA's NIM fell 50bp year-on-year to approximately 5.3% in 1H26, and the pattern is sector-wide. The mechanism has several components: policy rates held high to defend the currency keep funding costs elevated; competition for deposits from digital banks offering materially higher rates raises the marginal cost of funds; and asset yields have fallen as the mix shifts toward lower-yielding corporate lending and away from high-yield consumer credit. Sell-side commentary suggests NIM stabilisation may be underway, and BCA management guided to second-half improvement, but the direction of travel through 1H26 was unambiguous.

### Asset quality

System asset quality is deteriorating modestly from a strong base. OJK data showed consumption-loan NPLs rising from 1.9% in 2024 to 2.4% by October 2025, with increases also in working-capital loans. The consumer stress is concentrated in auto and unsecured lending, consistent with the roughly 20% contraction in BCA's auto book. BCA's own metrics remain far better than system averages — NPL approximately 1.9% and LAR approximately 4.9% at 1H26 — but the trend is mildly negative, and the sector-wide pattern indicates a genuine consumer credit cycle rather than idiosyncratic weakness.

### Regulatory environment

OJK supervises prudential regulation; Bank Indonesia handles monetary policy and payment systems. Capital requirements follow Basel III as implemented locally, with KBMI tiering determining permitted business scope. The Deposit Insurance Corporation (LPS) guarantees deposits up to a statutory limit and sets the guaranteed-rate ceiling that anchors deposit pricing.

Two regulatory themes bear watching. First, the payments regime — BI has driven interoperability through QRIS and the BI-FAST real-time transfer system, which structurally erodes the proprietary advantage of any single bank's payment network, including BCA's. Second, the digital-bank licensing regime that enabled SeaBank, Bank Jago, Superbank and others has permanently raised deposit-market competitive intensity.

### Structural trends

Three trends define the medium term. **Digital substitution** continues, with mobile and internet transaction frequency growing 19% year-on-year at BCA — a cost opportunity and a competitive threat simultaneously. **Ecosystem banking** is consolidating, as banks embed into e-commerce, ride-hailing and social-commerce platforms; the fintech-backed digital banks lead here by construction. **Financial deepening** remains the long-run driver: Indonesian credit penetration is low by regional standards, which underwrites a multi-decade growth runway for the sector as a whole.

## 7. COMPETITIVE LANDSCAPE

### The KBMI 4 incumbents

**Bank Rakyat Indonesia (BBRI)** is BCA's largest competitor by assets and the dominant microfinance franchise in Indonesia, with unmatched rural reach. Its economics are the mirror image of BCA's: it lends to micro-borrowers at very high yields, generating a NIM guided at 7.4–7.8% for 2026, but bears correspondingly high credit costs, guided at 2.9–3.2%. In 1H26 BBRI grew consolidated loans and financing 16.2% year-on-year to Rp1,646tn with net profit up 17.5% to Rp31.2tn — substantially outgrowing BCA on both loans and earnings. BBRI's vulnerability is credit-cost sensitivity: its model depends on micro-borrower resilience, and its returns compress sharply when that cycle turns.

**Bank Mandiri (BMRI)** is the largest by assets in some measures and the leading corporate and wholesale bank, strong in state-linked and large-corporate lending. It is BCA's most direct competitor in the corporate segment where BCA is now growing fastest. It grew loans approximately 21% year-on-year as of May 2026 — far above BCA's approximately 5% at the same point — though rapid growth in a decelerating economy raises questions about underwriting standards that will only be answered in the next credit cycle. Its NIM is materially lower than BCA's, reflecting a wholesale-weighted book and a less advantageous funding mix.

**Bank Negara Indonesia (BBNI)** is the smallest of the four, with a corporate and international orientation. It guides 2026 loan growth of 8–10%, NIM of 3.5–3.8%, and cost of credit of 1.0–1.2%. It grew loans approximately 25% year-on-year as of May 2026. Its returns lag the group — ROE materially below peers — and it trades at the lowest valuation of the four, roughly at or below book.

The P/B and ROE figures are as sourced and are point-in-time; all four require re-verification against live market data for Task 3, particularly given the share-price discrepancies noted in Section 10.*

The table captures the investment case and its principal risk in one line. BCA earns a peer-leading ROE with a mid-range NIM — meaning the return is generated by funding cost and credit quality, not by charging borrowers more — and the market pays roughly 60% more per unit of book value than for BBRI and 80% more than for BMRI. That premium is defensible only for as long as the CASA advantage and the credit-quality gap persist.

### Sharia and mid-tier competitors

**Bank Syariah Indonesia (BRIS)** leads Islamic banking, a structurally growing segment in the world's largest Muslim-majority country. **Bank CIMB Niaga (BNGA)** and **Bank Danamon (BDMN)** compete in commercial and consumer segments without matching the top tier's scale or funding cost.

### Digital banks

This is the most strategically significant competitive development, and it attacks BCA's deposit franchise specifically.

**SeaBank**, part of Sea Group (Shopee), has grown aggressively on high deposit rates and zero-fee positioning, reporting net profit of Rp678.4bn in 2025 on assets of Rp44.4tn and third-party funds of Rp34.8tn. **Bank Jago**, backed by GoTo and Singapore's GIC, integrates with the Gojek ecosystem. **Superbank**, backed by Emtek, GXS Bank (Singtel and Grab) and KakaoBank, grew assets 386% between 2023 and June 2026 — the fastest in the sector, against 88% at SeaBank and 95% at Jago. Earlier entrants **Bank Neo Commerce** and **Allo Bank** have stagnated, with Neobank's assets lower in June 2026 than in 2023.

The sector is consolidating around Jago, Superbank and SeaBank, and eight Indonesian digital banks reported profits in 2025 — the model is no longer purely subsidised growth.

The threat to BCA is specific and should not be overstated or dismissed. These banks are far too small to challenge BCA's Rp1,587tn balance sheet directly. What they do is compete for the marginal savings rupiah by paying rates BCA will not match, and their ecosystem embedding gives them customer-acquisition economics BCA cannot replicate. The realistic damage is not deposit flight but a gradual rise in BCA's marginal cost of funds and a slow erosion of CASA share among younger customers — precisely the mechanism that produces sustained NIM compression rather than a sudden shock. *blu* is BCA's hedge against this, and Central Capital Ventura provides early visibility.

### Competitive advantages and vulnerabilities

BCA's durable advantages are the CASA franchise and the cost of funds it produces; the transaction network, both physical and digital, that generates the CASA; underwriting discipline validated across two decades and one catastrophic prior failure; freedom from state-directed lending; and brand trust in a market where that is scarce.

Its vulnerabilities are the structural erosion of payment-network exclusivity through QRIS and BI-FAST interoperability; deposit-rate competition from digital challengers; a low-yielding asset mix that makes NIM sensitive to funding costs; complete geographic concentration in Indonesia; and a valuation premium that leaves no room for disappointment.

## 8. MARKET OPPORTUNITY

Conventional TAM sizing is a poor fit for a bank, where the relevant question is not the size of an addressable product market but the depth of credit intermediation in the economy and the share of it a bank can profitably capture. The analysis below is framed accordingly.

### The penetration opportunity

Indonesia's structural banking opportunity rests on low credit penetration. Domestic credit to the private sector as a share of GDP is substantially below regional comparators — Thailand, Malaysia, Vietnam and Singapore all intermediate far more credit relative to output. This gap, combined with a population exceeding 280 million, a median age in the twenties, and sustained GDP growth above 5%, means the Indonesian banking system should grow faster than nominal GDP for an extended period as financial deepening proceeds. That is the long-run engine, and it is genuine rather than promotional.

*This section requires quantification against World Bank and BI data for the final report: private-credit-to-GDP for Indonesia versus ASEAN peers, banked-population percentage, and mortgage-debt-to-GDP. These were not retrievable in this environment and represent a Task 1 gap.*

### Sizing the accessible market

The system-level parameters are as follows. Total system loan growth is running at approximately 9.4–9.7% year-on-year, with BI targeting 8–12% for 2026. BCA's loan book of approximately Rp1,036tn sits within a system whose four largest banks each carry books between roughly Rp1,000tn and Rp1,650tn.

BCA's serviceable market is narrower than the system total by deliberate choice. It does not compete in microfinance, where BBRI dominates and where yields and credit costs are both far higher. It does not take state-directed lending. Its addressable segments are large-corporate lending, commercial and SME, and mass-market consumer — a market that is large, growing with the economy, but where BCA competes with three well-capitalised state banks.

### Where growth actually comes from

The instructive fact about BCA's 2026 is that it grew loans approximately 5% year-on-year in May, below its own 8–10% guidance, while BMRI grew 21% and BBNI 25%. By June, growth had accelerated to approximately 8% as loans crossed Rp1,000tn. BCA is not capacity-constrained; its LDR leaves substantial headroom and its capital position is strong. It is choosing not to chase growth at prevailing risk-adjusted pricing.

This is the central strategic tension in the investment case. In a market where deposits are abundant and credit demand is adequate, BCA's binding constraint is its own risk appetite. That discipline is what produces the sector-leading asset quality that justifies the valuation premium — but it also means BCA will structurally underperform peers on loan growth during expansions and outperform during contractions. An initiation must be explicit that buying BBCA is buying a bank that will not maximise growth.

### Realistic medium-term trajectory

Absent a change in strategy, BCA should compound loans in the high single digits, roughly in line with or slightly below system growth, with mix shifting toward corporate. Deposit growth should track loans, with CASA holding at high but perhaps gradually declining share as digital competition bites. Fee income should grow with transaction volume, in the mid-to-high teens. NIM is the swing variable: stabilisation and modest recovery, as management guides, produces high-single-digit earnings growth; continued compression produces the low-single-digit growth seen in 1H26.

Net profit growth of approximately 4.9% in FY2025 and approximately 2% in 1H26 sets the empirical baseline. Any Task 2 model projecting a rapid return to double-digit earnings growth needs to specify precisely which of these variables inflects, and why.

## 9. RISK ASSESSMENT

### Company-Specific Risks

**1. Net interest margin compression.** NIM fell approximately 50bp year-on-year to 5.3% in 1H26 from 5.8%. On a Rp1,036tn loan book this is the dominant driver of the collapse in earnings growth to approximately 2%. The causes are structural as much as cyclical: elevated policy rates, deposit competition raising marginal funding costs, and an asset mix shifting toward lower-yielding corporate lending. Management guides to second-half improvement, but if compression continues, BCA's premium ROE — the entire basis of its valuation — erodes directly. *Severity: high. Likelihood: partially realised.*

**2. CEO transition execution risk.** Hendra Lembong became President Director in March 2025 after Jahja Setiaatmadja's fourteen-year tenure. BCA's valuation premium rests substantially on perceived management quality and strategic consistency. Lembong's background is IT, operations and international wholesale banking rather than Indonesian retail banking. Eighteen months in, results show continuity and no disruption. *Mitigant: Setiaatmadja's retention as President Commissioner preserves institutional memory. Severity: moderate. Likelihood: low and declining.*

**3. Consumer lending contraction.** Consumer loans declined year-on-year, driven by auto loans falling approximately 20%. This removes a high-yield component of the asset mix, mechanically compressing NIM, and signals genuine Indonesian consumer stress consistent with OJK data showing consumption-loan NPLs rising from 1.9% to 2.4%. Mortgages (+5%) and personal loans (+7%) only partially offset. *Severity: moderate. Likelihood: realised and ongoing.*

**4. Corporate loan concentration.** Corporate lending is approximately 49.6% of the book and growing fastest at 13.6% year-on-year. Growth is concentrating credit risk in fewer, larger exposures precisely as BCA competes for that business with BMRI. Corporate lending is also the lowest-yielding segment, so mix shift dilutes margin. Single-name and sector concentration data were not retrievable and constitute a genuine analytical gap. *Severity: moderate-high, unquantified. Likelihood: increasing.*

**5. Payment network disintermediation.** BI's QRIS interoperability standard and BI-FAST real-time transfer system are deliberately designed to make payments bank-agnostic. BCA's transaction network is the mechanism that generates its CASA, and regulatory interoperability structurally erodes the exclusivity of that network. This is a slow, non-reversible dilution of the moat rather than an acute threat. *Severity: high over a multi-year horizon. Likelihood: certain, already underway.*

### Industry and Market Risks

**6. Digital bank deposit competition.** SeaBank, Bank Jago and Superbank compete explicitly on deposit rates and ecosystem integration. Superbank grew assets 386% between 2023 and June 2026. None is remotely large enough to threaten BCA's balance sheet directly, but they set the marginal price of deposits and are disproportionately attractive to younger customers. The realistic damage is gradual CASA-share erosion and a rising marginal cost of funds. *Mitigant: blu by BCA Digital. Severity: moderate, compounding. Likelihood: high.*

**7. Consumer credit cycle deterioration.** System consumption-loan NPLs rose from 1.9% in 2024 to 2.4% by October 2025. BCA's own NPL rose to approximately 1.9% and LAR to approximately 4.9% at 1H26 from 1.7% and 4.8% at FY25. BCA's absolute levels remain excellent, but a genuine sector credit cycle is underway and BCA is not immune. *Severity: moderate. Likelihood: realised, early stage.*

**8. State-bank competitive distortion.** BMRI and BBNI grew loans 21% and 25% year-on-year respectively as of May 2026, against BCA's approximately 5%. State banks may operate under growth mandates and return expectations that differ from a private bank's, allowing them to price credit below levels BCA will accept. This costs BCA share during expansions. *Mitigant: historically vindicates BCA when the cycle turns. Severity: moderate. Likelihood: ongoing.*

**9. Regulatory and policy change.** OJK prudential requirements, LPS guarantee-rate ceilings, and BI payment-system mandates are all subject to change, and a bank of BCA's systemic importance is a natural target for policy that prioritises system objectives over shareholder returns. Directed-lending mandates, currently borne by state banks, could in principle be extended. *Severity: moderate-high if realised. Likelihood: low-moderate.*

### Financial Risks

**10. Valuation de-rating.** BCA trades at approximately 2.7x book against approximately 1.7x for BBRI, 1.5x for BMRI and 0.9x for BBNI. That premium capitalises the CASA advantage, the ROE gap and the asset-quality gap. With profit growth at approximately 2% in 1H26 and NIM compressing, the premium is vulnerable to a re-rating toward peers even if operations remain sound. This is the single largest risk to shareholder return and is largely independent of operational execution. *Severity: high. Likelihood: moderate.*

**11. Earnings growth stall.** Net profit grew approximately 4.9% in FY25 and approximately 2% in 1H26 — real growth close to zero against 2.62% projected core inflation. A bank valued as a compounder that does not compound is mispriced. *Severity: high. Likelihood: realised; the question is duration.*

**12. Credit cost normalisation.** BCA's cost of credit is low, reflecting genuinely conservative underwriting. Provisioning that has been a tailwind to earnings normalises upward as LAR and NPL drift higher, and the operating leverage runs in reverse. *Severity: moderate. Likelihood: moderate.*

### Macroeconomic Risks

**13. Single-country concentration.** BCA is a purely Indonesian bank with negligible international diversification. Every asset, liability and revenue stream is exposed to one emerging-market economy, its currency, its politics and its regulator. There is no offset. *Severity: high in a tail scenario. Likelihood: low but non-trivial.*

**14. Rupiah weakness and rate policy.** Bank Indonesia held rates at 4.75% for five consecutive meetings explicitly to defend a rupiah trading near Rp16,430/USD, rather than because domestic conditions demanded it. This keeps funding costs above where the domestic cycle would set them — directly worsening the NIM compression in Risk 1. Further currency pressure delays the rate relief BCA's margin recovery depends on. *Severity: high. Likelihood: realised and ongoing.*

**15. Fiscal and political risk.** The budget deficit is expected to widen to approximately 3.0% of GDP in 2026 on fuel subsidies and the Free Nutritious Meal programme. Fiscal expansion supports credit demand near-term but raises sovereign risk premia and crowding-out concerns. Danantara's capital deployment is a genuine upside case; the same vehicle also represents increased state involvement in capital allocation. *Severity: moderate. Likelihood: moderate.*
